Working name: Twige (to be confirmed by a Kinyarwanda speaker)

English and Kiswahili for everyday life and work

An adult course for confident communication at home, in the city and at work. A short level check first, then about 20 minutes a day in the language she chooses that day, a short review, one real task each week, a phrasebook of her own sentences, and progress shown as things she can now do.

The rhythm
ONCE, AT THE START
Level check
English: tenses, words, reading, listening, a spoken answer and a short message. Suggests a starting point; week 1 confirms it. Kiswahili starts at the beginning.
EVERY DAY · ABOUT 20 MIN
One lesson + review
She chooses English or Kiswahili for today. A lesson of about 15 minutes plus a 5-minute review. The other language is optional.
IN EVERY REVIEW
Recall, spaced
Words and sentences return on later days. She says or types them before seeing the answer.
EVERY WEEK
Real task
A voice note, an email, a phone call or a role-play from her own week, with specific, encouraging corrections.
EVERY 4 WEEKS
Check-up
A short re-check and a 2-minute recording, ideally heard by a person, so progress is real and audible.

English

Start set by the level check
Three strands take turns, using situations from an adult's real week.
Everyday life
Neighbours, the market and shops, the clinic, school and parents, transport and directions, the bank, landlords and repairs, phone calls, telling a story.
Work and business
Introductions, meetings, emails and messages, phone calls, customers and clients, prices and money, explaining a problem or delay, interviews and a CV, presenting an idea.
Grammar
The tense ladder, one step at a time, with comparisons to French or Kinyarwanda only where they help.
Tense ladder
1 · Present simple vs continuous
2 · Past simple (regular, irregular)
3 · Questions and word order
4 · Present perfect vs past simple
5 · Future: will, going to, present continuous
6 · Past continuous and telling stories
7 · Conditionals for offers and plans
8 · Passive and reported speech
Pronunciation
Shadowing in every lesson for rhythm and clarity. Sound drills are chosen from her own recordings: only the sounds that make her harder to understand.

Kiswahili

From the beginning → A2
Kinyarwanda comparisons where they genuinely help, such as umuntu / abantu ↔ mtu / watu. Every example is checked by fluent speakers before it goes in.
1 · Greetings and introductions
2 · Family and people
3 · Numbers, money and prices
4 · Market and shopping
5 · Matatu, directions and places
6 · Time, days and appointments
7 · Home, neighbours and repairs
8 · Bargaining and services
9 · Health and the clinic
10 · Work, school and officials
Who corrects what
The app, instantly: choices, gaps, word order, fixing sentences, listening questions and the review.
The app, listening (online): shows "I heard…" and offers another try. It is not a pronunciation grade.
AI feedback, later and optional: weekly voice notes and emails, each correction explained.
A person, occasionally: listens to the monthly recording and checks course content.
Her choice of language
Explanations in English, French or Kinyarwanda, changeable at any time.
Phone and laptop
Installs from a link, works offline, and keeps her progress on both.
First prototype
Level check, one English unit, one Kiswahili unit, review, phrasebook and voice recording.
Then
One week of real use, her feedback, and only then the remaining units.
